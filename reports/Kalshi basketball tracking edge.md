# Tracking Edge Favors College Pre-Game Lines

The most plausible place for a broadcast-video tracking pipeline to beat Kalshi is **pre-game NCAA men's basketball spreads and totals in conferences without in-venue camera systems**, with EuroLeague pre-game markets as a second, liquidity-starved laboratory and NBA in-game trading a distant third. The reasoning is structural: Kalshi's market makers (Susquehanna, Jump, Flutter, DraftKings) receive official data 1–4 seconds after the play while a TV or stream viewer is 6–60 seconds behind, so any in-game speed advantage is foreclosed; in the NBA the same makers' suppliers already hold Hawk-Eye 29-point skeletal tracking, so broadcast-derived coordinates are strictly worse data than what the other side of the trade uses. College basketball and European leagues are the only competitions where Kalshi has real two-sided volume (NCAA men's game markets carried ~150,000 contracts on 200 early-November games at 4.5-cent spreads) and where no positional feed reaches any sportsbook or data supplier. Even there, the honest expectation is narrow: no peer-reviewed study shows tracking-derived features beating a closing line, NBA closing markets are efficient to within the vig, Kalshi takers lose about 1.12% per trade on average, and a realistic, well-validated model should be budgeted at **1–3% ROI on turnover after fees** with capacity measured in hundreds of dollars per market early in the season. The edge must be earned by modeling (pace, shot quality, lineup context) rather than by data access, posted as maker orders rather than crossed as taker, and validated by no-vig closing-line value over several hundred bets before any P&L claim, since P&L alone needs roughly 3,000–9,000 bets to distinguish a 3–5% edge from luck. Jurisdiction matters too: sports contracts are geofenced off in Nevada, Michigan and Washington and are legally contested in roughly 21 states pending a Supreme Court petition.

## Kalshi lists 721 basketball series, but depth lives in NBA winners and futures

Kalshi's public API showed **4,076 sports series on 2 November 2026, 721 of them tagged basketball**, behind only soccer's 1,470; football had 602, baseball 236, tennis 144, golf 126 and hockey 108 ([Kalshi public API, series endpoint](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series?category=Sports)). For the NBA the menu reaches sportsbook parity: game winner, spread, total, team total, win margin, overtime, first basket, race-to-N, every quarter and half (winner, spread, total, team total), more than fifteen player-prop stat families (points, rebounds, assists, threes, blocks, steals, PRA and combinations, double-doubles, ladders, "protected" variants, head-to-head props), pre-packaged and user-built Combos, and a long tail of futures, awards and specials ([Kalshi public API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series?category=Sports); [SI, Oct 23 2026](https://www.si.com/prediction-markets/reviews/kalshi-nba)). WNBA has game, spread, total, team total, all quarters and halves, and props for points, rebounds, assists and threes; NCAA men's has game, spread, total, team total, halves, win margin, race-to-points and same-game-parlay combos but **no per-player props**; NCAA women's has game, spread, total and every quarter and half, also without props ([Kalshi public API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series?category=Sports)). Roughly forty international competitions are listed: EuroLeague has winner, spread, total and a champion market, as do China's CBA, Korea's KBL and Mexico's LNBP, while EuroCup, FIBA competitions, ACB, BBL, Italian Serie A, Turkish BSL, Lithuanian LKL, Japan's B.League, Australia's NBL and a dozen smaller leagues mostly carry game-winner markets only; **there is no G League series** ([Kalshi public API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series?category=Sports)). The menu was built in stages: single-game moneylines in January 2025, spreads and totals from August 2025, Combos in beta September 2025 and fully in December 2025, and NBA player props on 14 November 2025 for about fifty players with a $10,000 per-trade cap and an IC360 integrity partnership ([Front Office Sports, Aug 18 2025](https://frontofficesports.com/kalshi-adds-point-spreads-props-parlays-amid-legal-uncertainty); [Front Office Sports, Nov 14 2025](https://frontofficesports.com/article/kalshi-adds-nba-prop-markets-as-betting-crackdowns-surge/); [Covers, Dec 16 2025](https://www.covers.com/industry/kalshi-combo-event-contracts-raises-prediction-market-stakes-december-16-2025)).

In-game trading is not a separate product but the default state of every contract: game markets close two to three days after tip, so they trade continuously through the game until settlement, and Blockworks estimates that **about 85% of Kalshi sports volume is in-game** ([Kalshi public API KXNBAGAME](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXNBAGAME&status=open); [Blockworks Research, Apr 16 2026](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)). The in-game prop structure carries explicit regulatory risk: the CFTC's 267-page proposal of 10 June 2026 would permit most sports contracts but likely bar "discrete-action contracts involving specific participants," the category usually called in-game props ([ESPN, June 10 2026](https://www.espn.com/espn/betting/story/_/id/49019930/cftc-proposes-rules-limiting-prediction-markets-kalshi-sports)).

Basketball is Kalshi's second sport by dollars and briefly its first: in February 2026 men's college basketball traded **$2.27 billion against $1.74 billion for the NBA and $1.8 billion for the NFL**, and the 2026 NCAA tournaments drew more than $2 billion, with the men's title game alone at $89.3 million across winner, spread and total ([Front Office Sports, Mar 6 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/article/mens-college-basketball-was-kalshis-most-bet-on-sport-in-february/); [InGame, Apr 9 2026](https://www.ingame.com/2026-ncaa-tournament-volume-fees-kalshi/)). On the weekend of 24–25 October 2026 the NBA did $212 million on Kalshi on a Saturday versus $750 million for college football, and the top NBA game of the Sunday traded $15.2 million ([Covers, Oct 27 2026](https://www.covers.com/industry/kalshi-generates-10-billion-of-14-billion-in-weekend-trading-oct-27-2026)). That volume is extraordinarily concentrated, as the snapshot below shows.

### Fees punish 50-cent takers and reward makers on the thin markets

Kalshi's taker fee is **round-up(0.07 × contracts × P × (1−P))**, which peaks at 1.75 cents per contract at 50 cents and falls to 0.63 cents at 10 or 90 cents; the maker fee uses a 0.0175 coefficient (0.44 cents at 50 cents) but applies only on series flagged with maker fees ([BettingUSA Kalshi fees](https://www.bettingusa.com/prediction-markets/reviews/kalshi/fees/); [dev.to fee explainer](https://dev.to/polytrage/kalshis-fee-structure-explained-55lg)). Per the API, only 107 of 4,076 sports series charge makers at all; among basketball those are the NBA, NCAA men's and WNBA game, spread and total series plus major futures and awards, while **NBA props, quarter and half markets, NCAA women's and every international league including EuroLeague are maker-fee-free** ([Kalshi public API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series?category=Sports)). Because fees round up per order, a single contract at 50 cents pays 2 cents (4% of notional) versus 1.75% for 100 contracts, so small retail clips are penalized. There are no maker rebates or volume-tiered fees for ordinary users; the Volume Incentive Program excludes members under market-maker agreements, and makers' agreements separately include "discounts on fees, rebates on fees, revenue share" and order-protection tools ([Kalshi regulatory notice, Aug 30 2025](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/regulatory/notices/Volume%20Incentive%20Program%20-%20August%2030,%202025.pdf); [The American Prospect, Aug 26 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)).

The arithmetic for a retail model is unforgiving. Buying at a fair 50 cents as taker and holding to settlement needs a **51.75% true probability to break even**, marginally better than the 52.38% a −110 sportsbook line demands; but lifting a one-cent-wide offer at 51 raises break-even to 52.75%, worse than the book, and a round trip in and out as taker near 50 cents costs about 3.5 cents, 7% of stake ([Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets); [Karl Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)). Posting as maker on a flagged series costs 0.44 cents (break-even about 50.44%) and nothing on the unflagged ones, which is why every recommendation below is maker-side. The independent price checks are sobering regardless of fee structure: Citizens found Kalshi NFL moneylines and totals 8% worse than FanDuel and 6% worse than DraftKings including fees, and a 52-game NBA comparison found Kalshi had the best price on 0 of 104 sides once its fee was included ([CDC Gaming / Citizens](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/kalshi-pricing-remains-worse-than-sportsbooks-says-citizens/); [CoinCodex](https://coincodex.com/article/94502/the-nba-just-partnered-with-kalshi-and-polymarket-heres-how-their-odds-compare-to-sportsbooks)). The Roosevelt Institute estimates takers lost $584 million on Kalshi from launch to mid-May 2026, and an academic study found **takers lose 1.12% per trade while makers gain 1.12%** ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)). Kalshi's compensating advantage is capacity without limiting: CEO Tarek Mansour contrasts it with sportsbooks where "you get limited," and no documented case of Kalshi restricting a profitable account was found, though position accountability levels of $25,000 per strike apply and program makers are exempt from position limits ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/article/an-hour-with-kalshi-ceo-tarek-mansour-131979); [OddsShopper position limits](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-position-limits)).

Access is uneven. Sports contracts are geofenced off in **Nevada (since 12 August 2026), Michigan (injunction 1 September, $500,000/day penalty) and Washington (order 13 August)**; Ohio, Tennessee, Utah, Iowa and Connecticut have rulings letting states enforce; New Jersey and Arizona have injunctions protecting Kalshi; and the Ninth and Sixth Circuits' holdings that the contracts are gambling, not swaps, created a split now before the Supreme Court with Kalshi's response due 9 November 2026 ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/270741/kalshi-agrees-to-use-geofence-to-block-sports-contracts-in-nevada/); [Spectrum News, Sept 2 2026](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/mi/michigan/news/2026/09/02/michigan-judge-blocks-kalshi); [CNBC, Sept 25 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/appeals-court-rules-states-can-regulate-sports-prediction-markets.html); [OddsShopper state tracker](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/where-is-kalshi-legal)). A trader in a contested state should assume sports contracts can be switched off with little notice.

## Official optical tracking reaches sportsbooks only in the NBA

The question of where broadcast-derived tracking adds information reduces to: which competitions already deliver positional data to the people pricing Kalshi? The answer is narrow. In the NBA, Sony's Hawk-Eye has been the league's optical provider since 2023–24, with **14 cameras per arena capturing 29 body points per player and the ball at sub-second latency**; Sportradar, as the NBA's exclusive data provider, produces tracking datasets alongside Hawk-Eye, Genius/Second Spectrum retains the team-analytics and League Pass augmentation roles with all official tracking integrated into its engine, and Sportradar's "Virtualized Live Match Tracker" turns the feed into sub-one-second 3D visualizations explicitly built for in-game wagering ([NBA PR](https://pr.nba.com/nba-sony-hawk-eye-innovations-partnership); [INFORMS OR/MS Today, 2026](https://pubsonline.informs.org/do/10.1287/orms.2026.01.04/full/); [Sportico via Yahoo](https://sports.yahoo.com/genius-sports-nba-team-dragon-105500264.html); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/nba-sportradar-new-player-tracking-technology)). The NBA now even publishes Hawk-Eye-derived Shot Difficulty, Gravity and Defensive Box Score metrics on NBA.com and broadcasts ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-aws-partnership)). Whether raw positional data (as opposed to derived markets and visualizations) reaches sportsbooks could not be verified, but Genius supplies official data to more than 400 operators and the Sportradar–Kalshi deal lets Sportradar contract directly with Kalshi's market makers, so the working assumption must be that the counterparty in an NBA market has better tracking than any broadcast pipeline can produce ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/genius-sports-betting-advanced-cross-selling-november-2022); [Sportradar content hub](https://sportradar.com/content-hub/news/sportradar-and-kalshi-announce-first-of-its-kind-data-and-infrastructure-global-partnership-for-prediction-markets/)).

## Broadcast delay of 6–60 seconds forecloses every in-game speed play

A retail trader watching television cannot be ahead of Kalshi's in-game price on anything the official feed records. Stats Perform's Super Bowl LX study, the first to measure broadcast TV against streams with in-stadium spotters, put **over-the-air at ~19 seconds behind the field, cable at 38 seconds, Peacock 48, YouTube TV and Hulu Live 53, DirecTV Stream 60 and NFL+ 62**, with a 126-second spread among YouTube TV users ([TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/platform/study-ota-broadcasts-offered-lowest-latency-of-super-bowl-lx-feeds); [TheDesk.net](https://thedesk.net/2026/02/stats-perform-phenix-latency-super-bowl-lx/)). Even the older, more favorable figures (cable 6–8 seconds; the NBA app cut to ~12 seconds in 2022) are slower than official feeds, where Sportradar classifies anything under 4 seconds as "low" latency and reports sub-second venue-to-client delivery ([Front Office Sports, Oct 20 2022](https://frontofficesports.com/article/nba-streaming-latency-nba-app/); [Sportradar docs](https://docs.sportradar.com/live-data/latency-indicator-beta); [Fairplay Sports Media](https://www.fairplaysportsmedia.com/insights/sports-data-infrastructure/sports-betting-data-feed-integration-technical-guide)). No independent 2026 measurement exists for regular-season NBA or college broadcasts specifically, so the honest range for a trader's disadvantage is 5–55 seconds.

The other side of the book is fast and well equipped. Susquehanna has been Kalshi's flagship institutional market maker since April 2024, Jump Trading doubled its prediction-market team in July 2026, and Flutter and DraftKings make markets too; Kalshi's own affiliate handles under 6% of making volume ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240403664852/en/Kalshi-Onboards-Its-First-Dedicated-Institutional-Market-Maker); [The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/); [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/class-action-suit-against-kalshi-market-makers/)). Since June 2026 Kalshi has an official Sportradar feed for pre-game and in-play data, and since August 2026 a Level 2 order-book feed over dedicated fiber for institutions ([SBC Americas, June 8 2026](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/06/08/sportradar-prediction-markets-kalshi/); [The Block, Aug 12 2026](https://theblock.co/news/markets/2026-08-12-kalshi-first-prediction-market-to-stream-full-order-books-via-doublezero-411589)). The UCLA study of 173 Polymarket NBA games found every valid single-market arbitrage occurred in-game and lasted a **median 3.6 seconds** before automated participants closed it, with total capped profit of $210 across the whole month; Polymarket defends its makers with a 3-second (1-second on NBA, in testing) delay on marketable orders, while no Kalshi taker delay was found, meaning Kalshi relies on makers' own order-protection tools and quote-pulling ([arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1); [Polymarket help: Limit orders](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364444-limit-orders)). The practical consequence is that a retail trader's own resting in-game orders are the stale liquidity that faster participants pick off.

What in-game inefficiency survives is not about speed. The Knicks' 29-point comeback in NBA Finals Game 4 (June 2026) traded at about 5% and stayed under 10% for more than 80 minutes of real time before winning, costing makers $22.4 million pre-fee in the winner market and handing Susquehanna its largest sports loss; that was a modeling error on an extreme state, not a latency one ([InGame, June 11 2026](https://www.ingame.com/susquehanna-biggest-sports-kalshi-volume-knicks/)). More systematically, Angelini and De Angelis find that in a real-time binary prediction market a one-minute change in an out-of-sample benchmark probability moves price only **about 0.64-for-1**, with the unabsorbed portion predicting drift over the following several minutes, and the underreaction largest when liquidity is thin and for salient signals; their Kalshi NBA pre-game calibration runs Brier 0.204 at 24 hours to 0.199 at close ([arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07811)). Blockworks' case games show Kalshi tracking FanDuel at correlation 0.996 in-game with median gaps of 1.6–2.6 points that exceeded the 2.75% combined friction in 29–40% of five-minute windows ([Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)). The Betfair soccer literature adds that exchange prices respond correctly to expected goals but overreact to surprises, an effect that persists for minutes rather than seconds ([University of Reading](https://research.reading.ac.uk/football-research/new-journal-article-informational-efficiency-and-behaviour-within-in-play-prediction-markets/)). A multi-minute drift is compatible with a 20–40-second broadcast delay, so a tracking-informed live win-probability model is not foreclosed in principle; it is merely squeezed between a 3.5-cent round-trip taker cost, one-cent ticks and roughly $4,000 of touch depth, against counterparties who see Hawk-Eye data.

## No published model beats basketball closing lines with tracking features

The evidence that any model beats these markets is thin, and the evidence for tracking features specifically is absent. NBA closing spreads and totals test as efficient in the Even-Noble framework, with the lone exception of 10-plus-point home underdogs; early-season totals are biased (a strategy against the closing total early in the season won 56.72%, and Week 1 unders hit 58.2% over 2009–2012) but the authors stress no profitable strategy exists at the closing line in general ([The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612307000177); [UBPLJ](https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/article/view/601)). Opening lines around player absences carry exploitable bias that is gone by close, which is why practitioners measure skill by the open-to-close move ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612315000227)). Karl Whelan's 3,670-game NBA moneyline dataset is the clearest warning: about half of all bets had positive closing-line value, yet **three of five positive-CLV deciles did not profit, a +5% CLV decile returned +0.2%, and only the top decile (+11.4%) profited meaningfully**, because the ~4.5% margin must be cleared, not merely positive CLV ([Karl Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)). College basketball shows statistically significant weak-form inefficiency in one cross-sport study where the NBA did not, though Colquitt, Godwin and Caudill found the evidence limited; Whelan notes early college underdog bets earn CLV that closing odds are too poor to convert; the WNBA rejects efficiency at the 10% level without any simple strategy earning significant returns ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365618651_On_the_Efficiency_of_Sports_Betting_Markets); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-5957.00372); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/2/2/193)). The one paper reporting double-digit ROI across NBA, NCAAB and WNBA optimized its thresholds in-sample and has no true holdout ([arXiv 1910.08858v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.08858v2)).

On tracking features the published record is negative or silent for betting purposes. The only in-game tracking features with documented value (defender distance, shooter velocity, Second Spectrum's qSQ shot quality) operate at the shot and possession level, and no Sloan, JQAS or arXiv paper tests them against a closing line for sides or totals ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/nba/playoffs/2016/story/_/id/15530589/what-advanced-tracking-data-reveals-nba-shooters); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3050544525000040)). Stats Perform's tracking model out-predicts play-by-play for whether college players reach the NBA (log-loss 0.30 versus 0.40), which is talent prediction, not game prediction ([Sloan Sports Conference](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/predicting-nba-talent-from-enormous-amounts-of-college-basketball-tracking-data)). The best-studied tracking input, fatigue via speed and distance, yields statistically significant coefficients with a **largest adjusted R² of 0.0013**, while the schedule effects that do move net rating (+0.35 per day of rest difference, −1.29 for a third game in four days, −1.74 for three hours of westward travel) need no tracking data and are well known to books ([ar5iv 2112.14649](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.14649)). Props are the segment most plausibly mispriced, with average margins of 7.93% versus 4.77% on head-to-head and limits as low as $100 at books, but Polymarket's 5,074 NBA prop markets averaged 13 trades and $253 of volume each, and the NBA has asked books to drop unders on two-way and 10-day players, removing the minutes-driven class where a rotation model would have mattered most ([Low Juice Sportsbooks citing Mendeley 2026](https://lowjuicesportsbooks.com/prop-market-margins-in-mlb-and-nba/); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/how-sportsbook-limits-work-behind-the-scenes-july-14-2025); [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1); [Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/nba-asks-sportsbooks-not-to-offer-unders-on-2-way-or-10-day-players)).

The implication is that a pipeline producing player coordinates from broadcast video is a feature generator whose value is unproven and must be demonstrated by the trader, league by league. The features most worth testing are the ones the official play-by-play does not contain and that bear on totals and spreads: possession-level pace (time from rebound to shot, transition frequency) as a totals input, shot-quality and defender-distance expectation to separate shooting luck from skill in team-strength ratings, lineup spacing and on-court combinations to price absences and rotations faster than a scout feed does, and minutes and substitution patterns observed before they appear in box scores. Each of these is public knowledge for NBA books via Hawk-Eye and is unavailable to anyone pricing EuroLeague or a mid-major college game.

## Ranked recommendation: college pre-game lines first, NBA live last

| Rank | Competition and contract | Timing | Why the edge is plausible | Why it is capped | Realistic net edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NCAA men's spreads and totals (and winners) in non-Pac-12, non-MAC conferences | Pre-game, maker orders posted 12–36 h before tip | No positional data reaches books; Kalshi's second-biggest sport with real two-sided volume; academic weak-form inefficiency in NCAAB; hundreds of games give sample size fast | Spread/total books thin early season (11c spreads, ~25–30 contracts per market); maker fee 0.44c; hundreds of broadcasts to process; NBA-trained models transfer poorly | 1–3% ROI on turnover if the model is genuinely good; capacity in the low hundreds of dollars per market early, far more in March |
| 2 | EuroLeague spreads and totals (CBA, KBL, LNBP as extensions) | Pre-game maker orders | Cleanest informational gap: Sportradar has data and video rights but no tracking through 2031; legal US streams; no maker fee | 12 open markets, ~2,100 contracts total, 17c spreads; percentage edge may be large but dollars tiny; use as a validation lab, not an income source | Potentially 3–5% ROI on trivial size; treat as proof-of-concept |
| 3 | NBA in-game winners, 1H and quarter markets | Live, resting orders at model prices during multi-minute drift | Measured 0.64 underreaction with predictable drift; Betfair-style overreaction to surprises; period markets maker-fee-free | Counterparties hold Hawk-Eye and sub-4 s feeds; 3.5c taker round trip; ~$4K touch depth; your resting orders are the stale liquidity | 0–2% and highly uncertain; only after rank 1 or 2 shows positive CLV |
| 4 | NBA and WNBA player props | Pre-game maker orders only | Widest spreads (18–29c) and highest hold; maker-fee-free; a tracking-derived minutes/usage model is the only prop input books may lack | Props at ~13 trades/$253 per market on Polymarket, similarly thin on Kalshi; CFTC proposal threatens in-game props; $10K per-trade cap at launch | Unknown; a fill at your price is rare enough that fills themselves are adversely selected |
| 5 | NBA pre-game winner, spread, total | Pre-game | Deepest, tightest market (2c spreads, millions of contracts) | Most efficient market in the literature; books' suppliers hold strictly better tracking; Kalshi takers lose 1.12%/trade | Approximately zero; use only as the CLV benchmark |
| — | Any in-game speed play (score before the market) | Live | None | Foreclosed by 5–55 s latency gap and 3.6 s automated repricing | Negative |

The first rank rests on the intersection of three facts established above: NCAA men's game markets already carried roughly 150,000 contracts across 200 early-November games at 4.5-cent spreads, climbing to $2.27 billion in a single month of February and $89.3 million for one title game ([Kalshi public API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXNCAAMBGAME&status=open); [Front Office Sports, Mar 6 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/article/mens-college-basketball-was-kalshis-most-bet-on-sport-in-february/)); no sportsbook or supplier receives NCAA positional data ([Genius newsroom](https://www.geniussports.com/newsroom/ncaa-and-genius-sports-expand-partnership-through-2032/)); and the published efficiency evidence is weaker for college basketball than for the NBA ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365618651_On_the_Efficiency_of_Sports_Betting_Markets)). Totals are the natural first target because pace and shot-quality features map onto them directly, and because early-season totals are the one line type with a documented bias even in the NBA ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612307000177)). The honest caveats are that the spread and total books were only 5–6 thousand contracts across 200-plus markets at the snapshot, that the maker fee applies on these series, and that the gap will narrow where Genius installs GeniusIQ (Pac-12 venues from 2026) ([Gaming Intelligence](https://www.gamingintelligence.com/?p=228012)). EuroLeague ranks second despite the purer data gap only because of liquidity: twelve open markets and 17-cent spreads cannot absorb meaningful money, so its role is to prove the pipeline moves CLV in a league where the counterparty verifiably lacks tracking ([Kalshi public API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXEUROLEAGUEGAME&status=open); [Sportando](https://sportando.basketball/en/euroleague-and-sportradar-extend-data-and-audiovisual-betting-rights-deal-through-2031/)).

How large can the edge realistically be? Whelan's deciles imply that on vigged sportsbook lines a mean CLV of about +5% corresponded to roughly 0% realized ROI and profit appeared above that ([Karl Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)). Kalshi lowers the hurdle for a maker: at 50 cents the break-even is about 50.44% on flagged series and exactly 50% on maker-fee-free ones, so a model that is truly 52% on a 50-cent line earns roughly 1.5–2 cents per contract, 3–4% of stake, before adverse selection ([BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/prediction-markets/reviews/kalshi/fees/)). Adverse selection is the tax that replaces the vig: resting orders fill disproportionately when the line is about to move against them, which is why the 1.12% maker gain in the academic study belongs to professionals with fast data, not to a retail poster ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)). A fair planning assumption for a model that genuinely adds information is **1–3% ROI on turnover**, with upside in March and in any league where the pipeline reveals something scouts miss, and the dollar ceiling set by displayed depth, not by the model.

Validation must come before capital, and the order matters. First, record the no-vig closing price for every market you would trade, using a sharp consensus (Pinnacle or an Unabated-style vig-free line) for sides and totals and Kalshi's own last pre-tip mid as a secondary benchmark, and compute CLV on every paper order at the price and time you would have posted ([Karl Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595); [Dealroom profile of Unabated](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/unabated_products)). CLV converges on skill in a few hundred bets because it compares two prices rather than a price to a binary outcome; **results alone need roughly 3,100 bets to confirm a 5% ROI, 8,700 for 3% and about 19,600 for 2%** at conventional power, so a season of college basketball can validate CLV but not P&L ([Karl Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595); [Sharpery](https://sharpery.vercel.app/research/clv-long-term-profitability)). Second, score the model's probabilities against no-vig closing probabilities on held-out seasons with Brier and log loss, with Kalshi's own 0.199 closing Brier on NBA as the bar to beat, and inspect calibration by probability bucket because the fee curve punishes mid-range mistakes most ([arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07811)). Third, run an ablation: the same model with and without the broadcast-tracking features, because if box-score pace and efficiency deliver the same CLV the pipeline is cost without edge. Fourth, paper-trade through Kalshi's demo environment and then live at minimum size, logging fill rates on resting orders, since a strategy that only fills when wrong shows positive CLV on paper and negative P&L in practice; the Basic API tier's ~20 reads and ~10 writes per second is ample for this ([docs.kalshi.com, Rate limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits)). Report everything split by league, by line type, by pre-game versus in-game, and by maker versus taker, because CLV pooled across segments hides the fact that it is usually earned in one.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. The pipeline's value is not that it sees the court faster than the market, because it never will; it is that in college basketball and European leagues it sees the court at all, when the people pricing those games are working from a scout's play-by-play. That is a genuine but temporary asymmetry, and its half-life is set by Genius's camera-array rollouts and by whether Sportradar or SkillCorner decide to sell broadcast-derived tracking to books, both of which are plausible within the 2026–27 season. The strategy that follows is a research program disguised as a trading plan: build the features, prove on EuroLeague and mid-major college totals that they move closing-line value, and only then size into the NCAA markets where Kalshi has depth.

The second shift is in what "edge" means on an exchange. On Kalshi the enemy is not a 4.5% vig but adverse selection against makers who hold better data, so the posture that gives a retail model any chance is to be the patient resting order on thin, maker-fee-free or low-fee pre-game books, never the taker crossing a live spread. If the ablation shows the tracking features add nothing beyond box-score pace, the correct conclusion is that the pipeline is a good computer-vision project and a poor trading signal, and the money is better left in the account earning interest.
