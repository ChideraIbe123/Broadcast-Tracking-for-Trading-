# Two Hundred Hand-Labelled Frames Unlock College Calibration

No public dataset anywhere carries court-geometry labels on NCAA broadcast frames, so the college court model has to be built from your own labels; the good news is that the evidence says **roughly 200 well-chosen frames from ten or more arenas, grown to 400–500 with model-assisted correction, is enough**, and the frames themselves are already on your disk. The entire inventory of basketball calibration data as of November 2026 reduces to Roboflow's 850-image NBA set and its forks (which you already train on), a handful of other NBA-broadcast and amateur fixed-camera sets on Roboflow Universe, DeepSportradar's 728 French-league fixed-camera views under a non-commercial licence, and three academic sets that actually contain broadcast or NCAA footage (Sha et al.'s 640-image College Basketball set, Nie et al.'s SportsFields, Tarashima's SFLNet) that were never released ([Sha et al. CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Sha_End-to-End_Camera_Calibration_for_Broadcast_Videos_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf); [Waterloo survey](https://openjournals.uwaterloo.ca/index.php/vsl/article/download/3542/4588)). The only college set with court landmarks is 92 fixed-camera frames of a Carnegie Mellon gym ([Roboflow Universe](https://universe.roboflow.com/keltons-workspace-tjkou/cmu-basketball-court-keypoints)). For tracking, SportsMOT's 80 basketball clips (15 train / 15 val / 50 test, 1280×720 at 25 fps, drawn from NCAA, NBA and Olympic YouTube broadcasts) are the whole public corpus of broadcast-view college tracking data, and they are CC BY-NC 4.0 with a no-redistribution clause ([SportsMOT GitHub](https://github.com/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT); [HF split files](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT/tree/main/splits_txt)). No downloadable weights were trained on college broadcasts except one GPL-3.0 homography regressor whose training data is private ([darkAlert](https://github.com/darkAlert/sports-field-homography)); KaliCalib, the only peer-reviewed basketball calibrator with code, was trained on fixed arena cameras in three French arenas ([KaliCalib](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07795)). On licences, every streaming service you would record from forbids it by contract, and Disney/ESPN+, Peacock and BTN additionally forbid using their content to train machine-learning models, while YouTube bars downloading and automated access without naming AI ([Disney subscriber agreement](https://www.disneyplus.com/welcome/subscriber-agreement); [Peacock terms](https://www.peacocktv.com/terms); [BTN terms](https://btn.com/about/btn-terms-of-use/); [YouTube terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)). The fastest credible path is therefore to keep the 33-point schema you already decode, label about 200 NCAA frames from the SportsMOT basketball clips in CVAT with your existing pre-labels loaded, fine-tune from your current weights pooled with the 850 NBA images, hold out three arenas as the test, and then let the fine-tuned model pre-label a second batch for correction. Sha et al. built a working college calibrator from 526 training images clicked on 10 games, and KaliCalib from 480 images with heavy augmentation, which brackets the effort ([Sha et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Sha_End-to-End_Camera_Calibration_for_Broadcast_Videos_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf); [KaliCalib](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07795)).

## Only 92 college frames anywhere carry court landmarks, and they are not broadcast

Roboflow Universe hosts roughly forty basketball court projects, and the de-facto standard is the one you already use: `roboflow-jvuqo/basketball-court-detection-2`, **850 NBA-broadcast images with 33 numbered landmarks under CC BY 4.0**, whose filenames in the Hugging Face re-export are 2025 NBA playoff games (Celtics–Knicks, Cavaliers–Pacers, Nuggets–Clippers) ([Universe page](https://universe.roboflow.com/roboflow-jvuqo/basketball-court-detection-2); [HF export API](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/koppolusameer/basketball-keypoint-detection-yolov8)). Roboflow's own RF-DETR keypoint tutorial describes it as providing "33 landmarks mapped to specific locations on a basketball court", but no coordinate table is published anywhere, which is why three independent repositories (Purgty, cs25m116, koppolusameer) and your own `BCD33_TO_HOOPS` decoder each reconstruct the index-to-court map by hand ([Roboflow blog](https://roboflow.com/blog/train-rf-detr-keypoint); [Purgty/Basketball-Homography](https://github.com/Purgty/Basketball-Homography)). The forks (`samet-mmrat`, `sameer-prasad-koppolu`) are the same 850 images, so they add nothing; the only other NBA-broadcast keypoint sets above 500 images are `cocojumbopremium/homography-enrqp` (860, probably a re-annotation of the same set) and `priver-one/basketball_courts` (1,120, undocumented schema), and the most recently maintained is `nbavideolabeling/nba-court-keypoint-detection` with 198 images updated in October 2026 ([cocojumbopremium](https://universe.roboflow.com/cocojumbopremium/homography-enrqp); [priver-one](https://universe.roboflow.com/priver-one/basketball_courts); [nbavideolabeling](https://universe.roboflow.com/nbavideolabeling/nba-court-keypoint-detection)). Each uses its own point layout, so pooling them means remapping schemas, not just concatenating files.

The large Universe sets are the wrong camera. The 8,639-image `statslane/court-rvqsa`, the 3,000-image `statslanes-workspace` set, the 2,280-image `nxt1/hs-basketball-court-nxt1` and the 861-image `ethan-liu/hs-basketball-court-keypoints` are all elevated fixed cameras in high-school or club gyms, and the `roboticflows-workspace` homography sets are fixed 4K club cameras ([statslane](https://universe.roboflow.com/statslane/court-rvqsa); [nxt1](https://universe.roboflow.com/nxt1/hs-basketball-court-nxt1); [ethan-liu](https://universe.roboflow.com/ethan-liu/hs-basketball-court-keypoints)). The one college entry, `keltons-workspace-tjkou/cmu-basketball-court-keypoints`, is **92 images of a Carnegie Mellon (Division III) gym from an elevated fixed camera**, not television production ([CMU set](https://universe.roboflow.com/keltons-workspace-tjkou/cmu-basketball-court-keypoints)). The one broadcast-style non-NBA set worth noting is `ortinss-workspace-zrabf/paraiba-basketball-court-detec`, 638 Brazilian league frames with a yolo26n-pose model at 97.4% mAP50, which is the cheapest source of a different broadcast look on a FIBA-marked court ([Paraíba set](https://universe.roboflow.com/ortinss-workspace-zrabf/paraiba-basketball-court-detec)). Two Universe projects do contain NCAA broadcast frames, but without any geometry: `ncaa-duke-player-detection/duke-ncaa-basketball-detection` (560 Duke TV frames with player, ball, hoop and referee boxes) and `college-basketball-computer-vision/basketball-court-view-detector` (611 college broadcast frames classified court-view versus not) ([Duke set](https://universe.roboflow.com/ncaa-duke-player-detection/duke-ncaa-basketball-detection); [court-view set](https://universe.roboflow.com/college-basketball-computer-vision/basketball-court-view-detector)).

Outside Roboflow the picture is thinner still. DeepSportradar-v1's camera-calibration task is the only research-grade ground truth: **728 image–calibration pairs (480/164/84 plus an 84-image secret challenge split)** rendered from the Keemotion/Synergy automated centre-line cameras at 37 LNB Pro A games in 15 French arenas, with full 3×4 projection matrices in centimetres rather than homographies or keypoints, released on Kaggle under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([arXiv 2208.08190](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.08190v1); [challenge repo](https://github.com/DeepSportradar/camera-calibration-challenge); [Dataset Ninja](https://datasetninja.com/deep-sport-radar)). EPFL's SportCenter offers 50,127 amateur phone-camera frames with homographies, "free of use for research purposes", though a survey flags its real availability as uncertain ([EPFL page](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/cvlab/data/sportcenter-dataset/); [Waterloo survey](https://openjournals.uwaterloo.ca/index.php/vsl/article/download/3542/4588)). TrackID3x3 provides manually clicked court keypoints on one frame per fixed camera for 3x3 games, CC BY 4.0 ([TrackID3x3](https://github.com/open-starlab/TrackID3x3)). The datasets that would have mattered most do not exist publicly: Sha et al. (Stats Perform) built their college set from 13 NCAA games, each in a unique venue, by having professional annotators click 4–6 correspondences on 30–60 frames per game, yielding 526 training and 114 test images, and the ProCC benchmark table records it as "Open: No" ([Sha et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Sha_End-to-End_Camera_Calibration_for_Broadcast_Videos_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf); [ProCC](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09807v1)). Nie et al.'s SportsFields basketball split (21 broadcast videos, 359 train / 130 test) and Tarashima's 1,232-image SFLNet set were likewise never released ([Nie et al. WACV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Nie_A_Robust_and_Efficient_Framework_for_Sports-Field_Registration_WACV_2021_paper.pdf); [Waterloo survey](https://openjournals.uwaterloo.ca/index.php/vsl/article/download/3542/4588)). Hugging Face, Zenodo, IEEE DataPort, Figshare and Kaggle add nothing: HF holds one re-export of the Roboflow set (tagged MIT on HF, CC BY 4.0 in its own README) and four small model repos trained on it ([HF data.yaml](https://huggingface.co/datasets/koppolusameer/basketball-keypoint-detection-yolov8/raw/main/data.yaml); [HF README.dataset.txt](https://huggingface.co/datasets/koppolusameer/basketball-keypoint-detection-yolov8/raw/main/README.dataset.txt)).

## SportsMOT's 80 basketball clips are the whole public corpus of college tracking

For player tracking the inventory is one dataset deep. SportsMOT is "240 sports video clips of 3 categories", filtered to 720p, 25 fps "official recording" broadcasts and cut by hand; its basketball portion is exactly **80 clips split 15 train / 15 val / 50 test** according to the official split files, averaging 845 frames and about 10 tracks per clip, annotated in MOT17 format with players only (no referees, bench, ball, team, jersey or court labels) ([SportsMOT GitHub](https://github.com/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT); [HF splits](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT/tree/main/splits_txt); [SportsMOT paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.05170v2)). The GitHub README names the sources as "Olymplic Games, NCAA Championship, and NBA on YouTube", while the paper credits MultiSports' "NCAA, Premier League, and Olympics", and no per-clip league label exists; your own inspection of the 15 val clips found NCAA and FIBA footage and no NBA, and the clip names embed YouTube ids (for example `v_-6Os86HzwCs_c001`) that let you classify the remaining 65 ([SportsMOT GitHub](https://github.com/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT)). The official Hugging Face repository is ungated as of 2 November 2026 and serves train.tar (6.70 GB), val.tar (6.59 GB) and test.tar (23.21 GB); the test tar carries images without ground truth, which makes its 50 basketball clips a court-landmark labelling pool even though they are useless for tracking evaluation ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT)). The paper reports basketball as its hardest category, baseline HOTA 66.17, which puts your fine-tuned RF-DETR Medium result of 0.554 HOTA on 15 val sequences in context: the gap is the detector and association, not the data ([SportsMOT paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.05170v2)).

The second NCAA source is older and weaker. Ramanathan et al.'s CVPR 2016 dataset was built from 257 NCAA games on YouTube with 14,548 four-second clips labelled for 11 events and per-frame player boxes with tracking ids; Google's publication page offers no download, a 2023 paper reports it "not available", and the only live copy is a University of Toronto course subset, a 2.6 GiB 7z (HTTP 200, last modified February 2017) holding about 50 clips per action at 20 frames per 4-second clip, that is, 5 fps sampling with boxes of tracker rather than human quality ([arXiv 1511.02917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.02917); [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/pub45489/); [Toronto handout](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~urtasun/courses/CSC2541_Winter17/project_1.pdf); [data.7z](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~namdar/sports_course/ncaa/data.7z)). It is a detector-pretraining supplement at best. Everything newer is the wrong camera: TeamTrack's basketball subset is fisheye and drone footage of Japanese university play, TrackID3x3 is fixed consumer cameras and drones at 3x3 games, and no 5v5 broadcast MOT successor to SportsMOT appeared in 2024–2026 ([TeamTrack](https://atomscott.github.io/TeamTrack/); [TrackID3x3](https://github.com/open-starlab/TrackID3x3)).

The supporting modalities are all NBA or European. For jersey numbers, E-BARD offers 3,633 manually labelled 16-frame player stubs under CC BY 4.0, derived from NBA footage, and Roboflow's `basketball-jersey-numbers-ocr` has about 3.6k crops from the 2025 NBA Playoffs; a Universe workspace named `cbb-analytics-computer-vision` hosts a "Jersey Number Annotations Close Up" project that was not readable this session ([E-BARD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GabrieleGiudici/E-BARD-JerseyNumberRecognition); [Roboflow blog](https://blog.roboflow.com/identify-basketball-players/); [cbb-analytics](https://universe.roboflow.com/cbb-analytics-computer-vision/jersey-number-annotations-close-up)). For re-identification the only basketball set is DeepSportradar's 18,703 LNB thumbnails over 436 training identities ([arXiv 2208.08190](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.08190)). For the ball, WASB-SBDT's basketball split annotates 275,328 NBA-broadcast frames with a single ball centre, the largest broadcast-basketball frame set with any label, though its data licence is unstated ([WASB-SBDT paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.05237)). And there is no dataset anywhere that pairs broadcast video with official positional data: the 2015–16 SportVU logs are the last open tracking release and have no video, Stats Perform's 650,000-possession NCAA tracking corpus is internal, and BasketballBench aligns 2,501 NBA 2025–26 clips to play-by-play but ships no boxes or calibration ([SportVU on HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dcayton/nba_tracking_data_15_16); [Stats Perform](https://www.statsperform.com/resource/predicting-nba-talent-from-enormous-amounts-of-college-basketball-tracking-data); [BasketballBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23435)). Court-plane supervision for college must therefore come from court landmarks you label, and every tracking component except the SportsMOT-trained detector carries a domain gap into college uniforms and arenas.

## No downloadable weights were trained on college broadcasts, except one GPL checkpoint of unknown provenance

Every public court-keypoint model traces back to the same 850 NBA images. The Hugging Face repos `koppolusameer/yolo11n-basketball-court-keypoints` (AGPL-3.0), `MTerryJack/Element-PitchCalib-Basketball-Court` (ONNX, metadata pointing at the Roboflow project), `AnzeZ/basketball-court-yolo11m-pose` (args.yaml names Roboflow v19) and the hobbyist GitHub weights from abdullahtarek and CourtVision are all this dataset or an unnamed one, and none publishes an accuracy number on broadcast footage ([koppolusameer README](https://huggingface.co/koppolusameer/yolo11n-basketball-court-keypoints/raw/main/README.md); [MTerryJack metadata](https://huggingface.co/api/models/MTerryJack/Element-PitchCalib-Basketball-Court); [AnzeZ args](https://huggingface.co/AnzeZ/basketball-court-yolo11m-pose/raw/main/args.yaml); [abdullahtarek](https://github.com/abdullahtarek/basketball_analysis)). Your own yolo26s-pose at 1280 px, pose mAP50-95 0.884 and 0.12 m median external error, is already better documented than any of them, so there is nothing to download that improves on it for NBA footage.

The single checkpoint with evidence of NCAA training is `darkAlert/sports-field-homography`, a UNet+STN homography regressor whose auto-downloaded weights are named `NCAA+v6-640x360-aug_unet-resnet34…CP_epoch8.pth`, under GPL-3.0 with no dataset released and no reported accuracy ([darkAlert](https://github.com/darkAlert/sports-field-homography)). It is a possible teacher for pseudo-labels, not a foundation: GPL-3.0 contaminates derived code, the training data provenance is unknown, and a direct regressor at 640×360 is a different architecture from the heatmap-plus-RANSAC pipeline you have validated. The `ianmparker/basketball-perception` repo (AGPL-3.0, created October 2026) reports an 18-keypoint court model with 4.04 ft median reprojection error but explicitly does not distribute the weights, and notes transfer fails to a fixed top-down high-school camera ([ianmparker](https://github.com/ianmparker/basketball-perception)).

KaliCalib is the only peer-reviewed basketball calibrator with public code and a stated licence (CeCILL 2.1, GPL-compatible copyleft): a ResNet-18 U-Net predicting heatmaps for **91 perspective-aware sampled court keypoints plus two basket heatmaps**, about 14.5M parameters, trained on only 480 DeepSportradar images with flips and colour jitter, reaching 126.61 cm test MSE on the six-point court-projection metric against 592.48 cm for the line-segmentation baseline, with 76.5% of test frames under one metre ([KaliCalib paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07795); [CEA-LIST/KaliCalib](https://github.com/CEA-LIST/KaliCalib)). Its authors flag that the training set "lacks a wide variety of arenas" and that images come from the court centre, and the DeepSportradar camera is a fixed centre-line arena system with no pan, zoom, cuts or score bug, so its weights are a domain mismatch for television ([KaliCalib paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07795)). Two ideas in it do transfer: dense perspective-aware keypoint sampling (worth about 30% in their ablation) and flips (another 30%), and the fact that 480 images sufficed for a single-domain model ([KaliCalib paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07795)). The soccer field-registration codebases cannot be retargeted cheaply: PnLCalib, No Bells Just Whistles, TVCalib and KpSFR each hard-code a soccer pitch model and ship soccer-only weights, with no documented template swap, so retargeting means rewriting the template and retraining the detector from scratch, which is exactly the work you have already done in `hoops/court` ([PnLCalib](https://github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib); [TVCalib](https://github.com/MM4SPA/tvcalib); [KpSFR](https://github.com/ericsujw/KpSFR)).

The most important fact for planning comes from your own benchmarks rather than the literature, because the literature has no numbers for any open method on NBA or NCAA broadcast video. Your NBA-trained n960 model yields usable calibrations on only 28% of NCAA frames and 46% of FIBA frames, the stronger s1280 model does worse off-NBA (15% and 23%) because it has learned NBA-specific landmark visibility, and pseudo-labelling 720p YouTube NCAA clips from the calibrator passed only 65 of 5,330 frames, with visible errors even among those. The one documented synthetic attempt, Rokken's Blender-rendered 33-keypoint set, exceeded 80% accuracy on synthetic images and "was not able to generalize to real-world images" ([J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/fss/39/0/39_453/_article/-char/en)). Transfer, pseudo-labelling and simulation have each been tried and each fell short; the remaining lever is real labelled college frames.

## Every streaming contract bars recording, and three of them name AI training outright

The footage question splits into what you can view and what you may copy. Full NCAA games are legitimately published on YouTube by the NCAA's March Madness channel (classic and recent tournament games, including the 1991 Duke–Kansas final) and by the ACC Digital Network, which posts full-game replays for 2024–25 and 2025–26 about two days after tip; mirror metadata shows these uploads as 1920×1080 files of 72–80 minutes ([March Madness channel](https://www.youtube.com/@MarchMadness); [ACC Digital Network replay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DWnd-iDnA1M); [IA mirror metadata](https://archive.org/metadata/youtube-DWnd-iDnA1M)). The ACC replays are the best source of current-season, standard sideline-camera Division I production. The Internet Archive holds far more, including a single "MCBK_2018-19" item with 179 Stadium-network games at 1080p totalling 1.27 TB and dozens of sibling items, but these are user uploads of copyrighted broadcasts with no licence, and the "CC BY 4.0" tag on some mirrored YouTube items is an uploader setting that the rights holder never granted, since YouTube itself refuses Creative Commons marking on any Content-ID-claimed video ([IA MCBK item](https://archive.org/details/MCBK_2018-19_201905); [YouTube licence help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797468?hl=en)). The sanctioned route to archive footage is Veritone, the NCAA's exclusive video licensing agent since August 2024; the NCAA's own guide says there is "NOT an option available for the general public/fans to purchase NCAA championships footage" ([SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2024/08/13/veritone-named-global-archive-of-record-and-exclusive-video-licensing-agent-for-the-ncaa); [NCAA guide](https://www.ncaa.com/_flysystem/public-s3/files/Quick%20Reference%20Guide.pdf)).

The contracts are uniform in prohibiting capture and diverge only on whether they say the words "machine learning". The Disney+/ESPN/Hulu Subscriber Agreement (updated 17 October 2025), which governs ESPN+ and the ESPN app and therefore SEC Network and ACC Network streams, limits use to "personal, noncommercial use", says "you may not record, copy, store, or redistribute any Content", bars DRM circumvention and automated extraction, and in section 3(b)(v) bars "fine-tuning, training, testing, benchmarking or validation of any artificial intelligence or machine learning tool" ([Disney subscriber agreement](https://www.disneyplus.com/welcome/subscriber-agreement)). Peacock (3 December 2025) prohibits "copy, download, stream, capture, reproduce, duplicate, archive, distribute" and the use of "any Content for the purpose of directly or indirectly training, developing, or improving ... any artificial intelligence tool, model, system, or platform" ([Peacock terms](https://www.peacocktv.com/terms)). BTN, which B1G+ links to (5 December 2025), says you may not "copy, data mine, scrape or in any way extract or use the Content ... for the purpose of training any artificial intelligence algorithm, system, model or tool" ([BTN terms](https://btn.com/about/btn-terms-of-use/)). YouTube's terms (15 December 2023) are narrower in wording but cover the same acts: content is for "personal, non-commercial use", you may not "access, reproduce, download" it except as the service allows or with written permission from YouTube and the rights holder, you may not use "robots, botnets or scrapers", and you may not collect "any information that might identify a person (for example, usernames or faces)" ([YouTube terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)). Screen capture or yt-dlp is trivially possible on every one of these; none permits it, and fair use is a defence to a copyright claim, not a release from a contract you accepted. NCAA.com and March Madness Live's own terms could not be fetched and remain unverified.

Two consequences matter for your specific use. First, the CC BY 4.0 on the Roboflow sets is real but shallow: it licenses the annotator's work, while the frames remain NBA and broadcaster copyright of "unclear redistribution rights", so you should treat those images as something you train on privately rather than republish ([HF export README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/koppolusameer/basketball-keypoint-detection-yolov8/raw/main/README.dataset.txt)). Second, the two best research assets, SportsMOT and DeepSportradar, are non-commercial, and the stated end use of this pipeline is a for-profit trading model. That is a judgement call rather than a settled question, but the conservative architecture is to keep SportsMOT and DeepSportradar as benchmark and validation material, train the production court model on CC BY and self-labelled frames, and, if the strategy proves out, replace the SportsMOT labelling pool with footage obtained under a written research or commercial licence from a conference, an athletic department (which owns its in-house stream productions) or Veritone. The earlier strategy report already established why college is the target market and that no sportsbook receives NCAA positional data; nothing in the footage inventory changes that, but the legal exposure of the footage pipeline is the one input that report did not price.

## Label 200 frames from ten games in the 33-point schema, then let the model label the rest

Ranked by time-to-a-working-college-model against credibility, the options collapse into one clear leader, two cheap supplements, and a tail of things that look attractive and are not.

| Rank | Path | Frames to hand-label | Tool | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Self-label NCAA broadcast frames in the existing bcd2 33-point schema, pooled with the 850 NBA images, fine-tuned from your current weights | ~200 first pass, 400–500 after a corrected second pass | CVAT (self-hosted) with your `prelabels.json` imported; Roboflow private project as the alternative | The only path with evidence behind it: Sha et al. 526 train images from 10 games; KaliCalib 480 images; your own transfer, pseudo-label and the published synthetic attempt all failed |
| 2 | Grow the set by temporal propagation inside clips and model-assisted correction | 0 new clicks beyond corrections | CVAT track mode or optical-flow propagation | SoccerNet 2025 GSR entries propagated keypoints with Lucas–Kanade; multiplies frames per clicked frame |
| 3 | Add the 638-image Paraíba broadcast-style set and the 198-image nbavideolabeling set after schema remapping | 0 | script | Cheap broadcast variety on FIBA markings; schema remap is a few hours |
| 4 | Tracking: SportsMOT train+val for the detector and associator (done), Toronto NCAA subset and Duke set as detector supplements, self-labelled college jersey crops later | small | existing pipeline | SportsMOT is NCAA-flavoured already; remaining gap is ReID/jersey, which no public college set covers |
| 5 | Pretrain on DeepSportradar with a KaliCalib-style dense keypoint head | 0 | KaliCalib ideas | Possible second-order gain; NC-SA licence and fixed-camera domain make it an experiment, not a dependency |
| 6 | Synthetic Blender/NBA2K renders, high-school fixed-camera sets, the CMU set, darkAlert's GPL weights, soccer codebases | 0 | — | Documented sim-to-real failure; wrong camera; copyleft and unknown provenance; no template swap |
| 7 | Buy or license: Veritone/conference footage, or a vendor's tracking | 0 | — | Cleanest legally; slow and priced for broadcasters; revisit once CLV is positive |

The first-ranked path needs four decisions, and the research settles each of them. The frame source is already on disk: the 80 SportsMOT basketball clips (train, val and the GT-less test tar) are 1280×720 broadcast footage, about 845 frames per clip, which is on the order of 65,000 frames spanning dozens of NCAA, FIBA and Olympic games, and the YouTube ids in the sequence names let you keep only the college clips ([SportsMOT GitHub](https://github.com/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT); [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT)). Your `scripts/select_label_frames.py` already pre-selects 200 diverse frames with model guesses, which is exactly the first batch. The ACC Digital Network and March Madness full games are the fallback for arena diversity and 1080p detail, with the YouTube-terms exposure described above and the advantage that YouTube's contract, unlike Disney's, Peacock's and BTN's, does not name model training.

The schema should stay the 33 numbered bcd2 points, because that is what lets the 850 NBA images and the two 33-point forks train jointly with the new frames and what your `BCD33_TO_HOOPS` decoder and line-support calibrator already consume; the court template, not the schema, carries the NCAA geometry, and your per-league geometry already handles the arc difference. KaliCalib's result argues for eventually adding dense perspective-aware points on top, but that is a second iteration ([KaliCalib paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07795)).

Sampling matters more than raw count. Sha et al. chose 13 games each in a unique venue, 30–60 frames per game, and held out 3 entire games as test; your college footage so far reaches only 28% usable frames precisely because arena floors, line colours and camera positions vary, and a Stanford project on NBA frames notes that "line colors aren't regulated and vary by venue" ([Sha et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Sha_End-to-End_Camera_Calibration_for_Broadcast_Videos_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf); [Stanford CS231A](https://stanford.edu/class/cs231a/prev_projects_2015/basketball.pdf)). So the 200-frame first pass should cover at least 10 games and arenas at 15–25 frames each, both court ends, wide and tight zooms, and transition frames where few landmarks are visible, with three arenas withheld entirely as the external test that replaces the 80-image NBA external set for college evaluation. KaliCalib reached a usable model from 480 training images with horizontal flips and colour jitter, and its ablation credits flips with about 30% of the error reduction, so the training recipe should keep heavy photometric and flip augmentation, pool the 850 NBA frames, and fine-tune from `court_bcd33_n960` or `s1280` rather than from COCO ([KaliCalib paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07795)). A fair target is to lift usable college frames from 28% toward the 98% your in-domain line-support pipeline achieves on NBA, with the held-out arenas measured by the same median-error and usable-frame metrics in `BENCHMARKS.md`.

The tool choice favours CVAT. CVAT's skeleton Configurator lets you define the 33 named points and their edges once, each point carries Outside and Occluded flags that are saved, skeletons can be annotated in Track mode on video, model-driven skeleton pre-annotation is supported through its AI Tools with per-point mapping review, and export is COCO Keypoints or CVAT-for-images ([CVAT skeleton docs](https://docs.cvat.ai/docs/annotation/manual-annotation/shapes/skeletons/); [CVAT blog](https://cvat.ai/blog/cvat-auto-annotation-skeletons)). Roboflow Annotate also supports a named 33-point skeleton with visible/occluded/skipped flags and COCO export, and it is what produced the bcd2 set, but its Label Assist documentation covers boxes and polygons only, so pre-labels would have to be uploaded as COCO JSON rather than generated in-tool, and a Roboflow workspace means uploading copyrighted frames to a third party ([Roboflow keypoint blog](https://blog.roboflow.com/keypoint-detection-on-roboflow/); [Roboflow Label Assist docs](https://docs.roboflow.com/annotate/model-assisted-labeling)). Label Studio is out: it has keypoints but no skeleton grouping and no direct COCO-keypoint export ([Label Studio community](https://community.labelstud.io/t/multi-object-in-image-keypoint-labeling-and-export/421); [MMPose guide](https://mmpose.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guides/label_studio.html)). Self-hosted CVAT keeps the frames local, accepts your `prelabels.json` as a starting skeleton per frame, and supports the second-pass workflow of correcting model output instead of clicking from scratch. No published labelling diary gives a time per frame for this task; budgeting one to two minutes per frame for a 33-point skeleton with roughly ten visible points, the 200-frame first pass is an afternoon to a day of focused work, and the corrected second pass to 500 is faster per frame. That is an estimate, not a measurement.

The second-ranked supplement turns clicked frames into many more. Frames sampled from the same clip share a slowly moving camera, so a clicked keyframe can be propagated to its neighbours by optical flow and then snapped by your line-alignment refinement; SoccerNet 2025 Game State Reconstruction entries did exactly this, initialising from a keypoint detector and tracking the keypoints with Lucas–Kanade across frames ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19182)). CVAT's Track mode gives the same effect manually. This is how 200 clicked frames become 1,000 or more training frames at the cost of some label correlation, which the arena-held-out test will expose if it is a problem.

On the tracking side the path is shorter because SportsMOT already is college footage. The remaining data gaps are team assignment, jersey numbers and re-identification in college uniforms, for which no public set exists; E-BARD and the Roboflow NBA OCR crops give a starting model under CC BY and unverified terms respectively, and a few hundred self-labelled college crops are the realistic fix once the court model is done ([E-BARD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GabrieleGiudici/E-BARD-JerseyNumberRecognition); [Roboflow blog](https://blog.roboflow.com/identify-basketball-players/)). The Toronto NCAA subset and the 560-frame Duke set are worth adding to detector training for arena and uniform variety, with the caveat that the Toronto boxes are 5 fps and tracker-quality ([Toronto handout](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~urtasun/courses/CSC2541_Winter17/project_1.pdf); [Duke set](https://universe.roboflow.com/ncaa-duke-player-detection/duke-ncaa-basketball-detection)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the problem from "find the college dataset" to "run the Sha et al. protocol yourself in a weekend". Every shortcut has now been tried by someone and documented as failing: NBA-to-NCAA transfer collapses to 28% usable frames in your own measurements, calibrator pseudo-labelling passed 65 of 5,330 frames, the one synthetic basketball court dataset did not generalise, and the academic groups that built broadcast or college calibration sets kept them private. What remains is unglamorous and small, and the two published data points (526 and 480 training images) say it is enough. The frames you need are already licensed for research on your disk in SportsMOT, the schema and decoder already exist, and the pre-labels already exist; the missing ingredient is a few hours in CVAT and a held-out set of three arenas to keep the result honest.

The deeper implication is about where the risk sits. Technically the college court model is now the least uncertain part of the pipeline. The uncertain parts are legal and strategic: the footage you would scale to (current-season ESPN+, Peacock and BTN streams) is contractually closed to recording and to model training, YouTube's official replays are closed to downloading, and the two best research datasets are non-commercial while the end use is a trading book. If the closing-line-value test in the earlier report comes back positive, the next spend is not more labelling but a written licence for footage, because that is the point at which a hobby-scale research corpus stops being defensible and a conference, a school or Veritone becomes the supplier.
