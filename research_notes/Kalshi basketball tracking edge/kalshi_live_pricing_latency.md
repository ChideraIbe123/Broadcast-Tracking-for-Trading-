# Kalshi Live (In-Game) Sports Pricing, Speed, and the Broadcast-Latency Question (as of 2026-11-01)

Scope note: These notes cover who prices Kalshi's in-game sports markets, what data they use, how fast official feeds are versus TV/streaming, documented latency-arbitrage/courtsiding, Kalshi's order-book and account-treatment rules, and the academic/industry evidence on prediction-market sports efficiency. Measured numbers are labeled as such; anecdotes and vendor claims are flagged. Research was conducted 2026-11-01; all dates below are the dates of the underlying events or publications.

---

## KQ1. Who makes markets on Kalshi sports contracts, and what data do they use?

### Takeaway
Kalshi's sports liquidity is dominated by professional market makers: Susquehanna (SIG, its first dedicated institutional MM since April 2024 and widely described as its largest), Kalshi's own affiliate Kalshi Trading (<6% of making volume as of Nov 2025), plus Jump Trading, Flutter/FanDuel and DraftKings per Aug 2026 reporting. Since June 2026 Kalshi has an official Sportradar feed (pre-game and in-play data and odds, with Sportradar allowed to contract directly with Kalshi's market makers) and since Aug 2026 a Genius Sports feed for soccer; Kalshi also launched a dedicated low-latency Level 2 order-book feed for institutions in Aug 2026. Market makers hold an explicit contractual position of fee rebates, position-limit exemption and "order protection" tools.

### Cited Findings
- Kalshi announced Susquehanna Government Products, LLLP (a member of SIG) as its "first dedicated institutional market maker" on April 3, 2024 — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240403664852/en/Kalshi-Onboards-Its-First-Dedicated-Institutional-Market-Maker)
- SIG "is believed to be Kalshi's largest market maker." In NBA Finals Game 4 (Spurs at Knicks, June 10, 2026), the Knicks came back from 29 down; market makers as a group traded $119.9M vs. takers' $87.0M in the game-winner market and takers made $22.4M pre-fee ($20.6M after fees), i.e., a $22.4M pre-fee MM loss. SIG called it its largest sports loss to date; Jeff Yass: "I won't confirm the exact loss, but it was a tough day." The Knicks traded at ~5% while down 29 in the third quarter and stayed below 10% for more than 80 minutes of real time. Kalshi total day volume was a record $872M; the game-winner market traded $171.6M with $2.3M in Kalshi fees — [InGame, June 11, 2026](https://www.ingame.com/susquehanna-biggest-sports-kalshi-volume-knicks/)
- Named market makers as of Aug 26, 2026: Kalshi Trading (affiliate), Susquehanna (since April 2024), Jump Trading ("doubled its prediction market team in July"), Flutter (FanDuel's parent) and DraftKings ("live on three exchanges"). Most prediction markets don't disclose their full MM list. DraftKings estimates institutional and professional bettors make up 80–90% of its prediction-market volume in legal-betting states — [The American Prospect, Aug 26, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)
- Kalshi's membership agreement lists "discounts on fees, rebates on fees, revenue share from fees" for market makers, plus "sophisticated risk management tools" such as order protections that "may give market makers a trading advantage" (quoting the agreement). Flutter reported $6M in Q2 2026 prediction-market revenue from market making and expects $50M for 2026 — [The American Prospect, Aug 26, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)
- Kalshi co-founder Luana Lopes Lara (Nov 26, 2025 statement): Kalshi Trading represented "less than 6%" of the platform's making volume the prior month; Kalshi "works with other market makers, though it has not named them." A class action (SDNY, filed late Nov 2025, Lieff Cabraser) alleges MMs get reduced fees, differing position limits and "enhanced market access"; Kalshi called it "meritless fiction" — [iGaming Business, Dec 2, 2025](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/class-action-suit-against-kalshi-market-makers/)
- A Yahoo Finance headline reports a Kalshi co-founder saying the in-house trading arm is "not profitable" (article body not retrieved) — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/kalshi-co-founder-says-house-174507266.html)
- Under Kalshi Rule 5.19(a), market makers in a Kalshi market maker program are excluded from position limits on the contracts covered by that program — [OddsShopper summary of Kalshi rulebook](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-position-limits)
- Members who have executed a Market Maker Agreement are excluded from Kalshi's Volume Incentive Program — [Kalshi regulatory notice, Aug 30, 2025](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/regulatory/notices/Volume%20Incentive%20Program%20-%20August%2030,%202025.pdf)
- Sportradar–Kalshi multi-year global partnership announced June 8, 2026: official data and live odds for pre-game and in-play markets (MLB, NHL, MLS, UFC named), fan-engagement content, customer acquisition, integrity monitoring; "allows Sportradar to enter into agreements directly with Kalshi's key partners, including brokers and market makers" — [Sportradar content hub](https://sportradar.com/content-hub/news/sportradar-and-kalshi-announce-first-of-its-kind-data-and-infrastructure-global-partnership-for-prediction-markets/); [SBC Americas, June 8, 2026](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/06/08/sportradar-prediction-markets-kalshi/)
- Genius Sports–Kalshi deal (announced ~Aug 5, 2026): official real-time data across Genius's soccer portfolio (Premier League, Serie A, Liga MX, Argentine Primera, Ligue 1), integrity information-sharing, and "certain market types will be restricted" to guard against manipulation — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/genius-sports-signs-kalshi-and-polymarket-as-us-prediction-markets-scale); [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/prediction-markets/genius-sports-enters-prediction-markets-polymarket-kalshi-partnerships/)
- Kalshi launched a real-time Level 1/Level 2 order-book feed for institutional traders via DoubleZero Edge (dedicated fiber, multicast) around Aug 12–13, 2026, covering sports event contracts and crypto perps; previously systematic firms "relied on REST APIs to reconstruct order book depth, adding latency" — [The Block, Aug 12, 2026](https://theblock.co/news/markets/2026-08-12-kalshi-first-prediction-market-to-stream-full-order-books-via-doublezero-411589); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/kalshi-launches-real-time-level-2-data-feed-for-institutional-traders)
- Kalshi's position (via a Kalshi official) is that market makers' role is to "react to pricing, not to set or influence it" — [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/class-action-suit-against-kalshi-market-makers/)
- Retail/taker outcomes: Roosevelt Institute estimate that takers lost $584M on Kalshi from July 2021 launch to mid-May 2026; an academic study (unnamed in article) found takers lost an average 1.12% per trade and makers gained 1.12%; Sportico: retail lost $117M on Kalshi combos to market makers Jan–Apr 2026 — [The American Prospect, Aug 26, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)

### Inferences
- The Sportradar deal's clause letting Sportradar contract directly with Kalshi's market makers implies that SIG/Jump/Flutter/DraftKings can (and, as sportsbook operators, already do) price Kalshi in-game markets from official low-latency venue data rather than from TV.
- The Knicks Game 4 loss shows MMs carry real directional risk in-game and can misprice badly (5% for a team that won), so "the MM is always right" is false; but that mispricing was a modeling/judgment error, not a latency one.
- The 1.12%/trade taker loss and the Jan–Apr combos figure are consistent with a market where takers (retail) systematically cross spreads against better-informed makers.

### Gaps
- No public statement from SIG, Jump, Flutter or DraftKings about which specific feed (Sportradar vs. Genius vs. league direct vs. their own in-venue scouts) powers their Kalshi NBA/college-basketball quoting. Not found.
- Kalshi's official market-maker-program page/terms for sports (rebate rates, quoting obligations) were not retrieved; only the membership-agreement language quoted by the Prospect and the CFTC filings' exclusions.
- The NBA's own official-data distributor arrangement for prediction markets (reporting elsewhere describes NBA deals with Kalshi and Polymarket in 2026, e.g., [CoinCodex](https://coincodex.com/article/94502/the-nba-just-partnered-with-kalshi-and-polymarket-heres-how-their-odds-compare-to-sportsbooks)) was not verified against a primary source in this session; the Sportradar–Kalshi release names MLB/NHL/MLS/UFC, not NBA.

## KQ2. Broadcast/stream delay vs. official data-feed latency (NBA, college basketball)

### Takeaway
Measured 2026 broadcast delays are far larger than commonly assumed: in Stats Perform's Super Bowl LX spotter study, over-the-air was ~19 s behind the field, cable 38 s, Peacock 48 s, YouTube TV/Hulu Live 53 s, DirecTV Stream ~60 s and NFL+ 62 s. Official data feeds classify "low" latency as under 4 s (Sportradar), with vendor claims of under 1 s venue-to-client. Even the most favorable older figure for cable (6–8 s) is slower than in-venue data. No independent per-platform measurement for NBA on ESPN+/Prime/Peacock regular-season games was found.

### Cited Findings
**Measured broadcast/stream delay**
- Stats Perform Super Bowl LX (Feb 2026) study, using real-time spotters comparing on-field action to each feed: broadcast (OTA) ~19 s; cable 38 s; Peacock 48 s; YouTube TV and Hulu + Live TV 53 s; DirecTV Stream 60 s; NFL+ 62 s. Largest spread across users: YouTube TV 126 s vs. Peacock 32 s and Hulu 3 s. First year Stats Perform measured broadcast TV against other platforms — [TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/platform/study-ota-broadcasts-offered-lowest-latency-of-super-bowl-lx-feeds); [TheDesk.net](https://thedesk.net/2026/02/stats-perform-phenix-latency-super-bowl-lx/); [Stats Perform PDF (403 on fetch; not read directly)](https://www.statsperform.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-Super-Bowl-%E2%80%93-Latency-Study.pdf)
- Comcast's own tests (Feb 2026): its RealTime4K Peacock-branded channel on Xfinity was 17 s behind on-field action; YouTube TV's low-latency feed 26 s (Comcast argued Stats Perform's 53 s may not have been the low-latency mode) — [NewscastStudio, Feb 20, 2026](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/02/20/xfinity-super-bowl-lx-latency/)
- Phenix (acquired by Stats Perform) earlier Super Bowl studies: Tubi ~26 s (SB LIX, 2025); Paramount+ just over 48 s (SB LVIII, 2024) — [TheDesk.net](https://thedesk.net/2026/02/stats-perform-phenix-latency-super-bowl-lx/)
- Stats Perform's first LX report erroneously listed a Tubi number; spotters had misreported YouTube TV's lag as Tubi — [TheDesk.net](https://thedesk.net/2026/02/stats-perform-phenix-latency-super-bowl-lx/)
- NBA app (League Pass + NBA TV), Oct 20, 2022: latency cut to ~12 s (production truck to phone) from a previous 30–40 s; article states broadcast/cable "typically run 6 to 8 seconds" and streaming can reach ~45 s. NBA SVP Ken DeGennaro: "For many years, the live streaming viewer has been disadvantaged from a timing perspective." The article (not an executive) says NBA/MLB can't launch in-game wagering products until latency nears broadcast levels. NOTE: article is from 2022, not 2026 — [Front Office Sports, Oct 20, 2022](https://frontofficesports.com/article/nba-streaming-latency-nba-app/)
- YouTube TV offers a "Decreased" broadcast-delay setting (reportedly trims 5–10 s); ESPN, Peacock and Paramount+ reportedly lack a comparable toggle (unverified secondary claim) — [Sportscasting (403 on fetch; from search snippet)](https://www.sportscasting.com/news/how-far-behind-is-streaming-from-live-sports-the-real-seconds-of-delay-explained/)
- Anecdotal forum reports: Xfinity users reported Peacock ~30 s ahead of their cable feed; a DBSTalk poster reported Peacock ~10 s behind local NBC on satellite (anecdotal, unmeasured) — [Xfinity forum](https://forums.xfinity.com/conversations/x1/why-is-live-tv-delayed-45-seconds/6525fa12f8fcd3407e3c627a); [DBSTalk](https://www.dbstalk.com/threads/delay-on-live-sports.231678/)
- ESPN+ NHL streams were reported to fall "minutes behind" live after ad-insertion breaks (anecdotal incident report) — [Russian Machine Never Breaks](https://russianmachineneverbreaks.com/?p=560669)

**Official data-feed latency**
- Sportradar Latency Indicator (Beta) levels for live data: Low ~0–<4 s; Moderate ~4–<8 s; High ~8–<12 s; Very High ~12–<16 s; Exceptional 16+ s. Assigned automatically from historical reference data for the expected coverage and source; pre-match level reflects expected broadcast/stream source, live level reflects observed conditions; examples on the page use `coveredfrom="tv"` — [Sportradar docs](https://docs.sportradar.com/live-data/latency-indicator-beta)
- A third-party article cites Sportradar's 2025 annual report as saying venue-to-client live data delivery is under one second (company-reported, not independently tested) — [Fairplay Sports Media](https://www.fairplaysportsmedia.com/insights/sports-data-infrastructure/sports-betting-data-feed-integration-technical-guide)
- Genius Sports is reported to state "under a second from in-stadia capture" for Football DataCo matches, with NFL streams carrying only a "low-latency" label and no figure (secondary comparison site) — [Partnerkin](https://partnerkin.com/en/b2b/betting-odds-api/)
- Polymarket's own docs say its displayed sports data is "for informational purposes only" and "may be delayed, contain errors, or omit recent events" — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/realtime-data)
- Anecdotal X posts (Jan 2026) give the stylized chain "Real event T=0; data feed T=+3 s; livestream T=+45 s" for Polymarket sports (anecdotal/promotional, not measured) — [@0xRicker](https://x.com/0xRicker/status/2008998834580427101); [@0xMovez](https://x.com/0xMovez/status/2008298059059343819)

### Inferences
- The Stats Perform cable figure (38 s) conflicts with the "6–8 s" conventional figure in the 2022 FOS piece. The likely reconciliation is methodology (spotters measuring from on-field action through the full production chain, versus glass-to-glass distribution latency only) and the Super Bowl's heavier production/profanity-delay chain; regular-season NBA on cable is plausibly closer to the low end, but no 2026 measurement for NBA was found.
- Under any of these numbers, a viewer on streaming (ESPN+, Peacock, Prime) is 30–60 s behind, a cable viewer roughly 6–38 s behind, and an OTA viewer ~19 s behind, while an in-venue scout or official tracking feed delivers inside 1–4 s. The gap in favor of feed-based market makers is on the order of 5–55 seconds.
- Streaming delay also varies across users of the same service (YouTube TV spread 126 s), so a retail trader cannot even assume a stable delay for themselves.

### Gaps
- No independent, measured delay for NBA or college basketball specifically on ESPN+, Peacock, Prime Video, Sling, Fubo or NBA League Pass in 2026. One site claims ESPN+ at 47 s but it is low-quality SEO content and should be discounted.
- Sportradar's Latency Indicator page does not state typical latency for in-venue scout coverage versus TV-sourced coverage; the sub-1-second claims are company-reported.
- Which NBA/college games are covered in-venue versus from TV by Sportradar/Genius (which determines feed latency) was not established.

## KQ3. Evidence of latency arbitrage / courtsiding against Kalshi or Polymarket, and countermeasures

### Takeaway
The best evidence is academic: a UCLA study of 173 Polymarket NBA games (Feb–Mar 2026) found every valid single-market arbitrage episode occurred in-game and lasted a median 3.6 s before "automated participants" closed it; combinatorial gaps lasted a median 16 s in-game. Polymarket's documented defense is a seconds-scale matching delay on marketable (taker) orders in sports markets (3 s general, 1 s being tested on NBA/MLB) and a 250 ms hold on crypto up/down markets. I found no documented Kalshi in-game taker delay, and no named prosecution or enforcement case of courtsiding on Kalshi or Polymarket. Historical courtsiding (tennis, Australian Open 2014) shows books respond with in-play bet delays and suspensions.

### Cited Findings
**Prediction markets**
- Cheng, Yang and Zou (UCLA), "Arbitrage Analysis in Polymarket NBA Markets," arXiv 2605.00864 (submitted Apr 22, 2026): 173 NBA games, Feb 4–Mar 4, 2026; 75,088,497 order-book snapshots. Single-market arbitrage (Ask_A + Ask_B < $1.00 or Bid_A + Bid_B > $1.00): 7 valid episodes, all in-game, median duration 3.614 s, zero pre-game. Moneyline–spread combinatorial arbitrage: 290 active episodes (11 pre-game, median 11.0 s; 279 in-game, median 16.0 s); 17.2% lasted ≤4.0 s. Corrections attributed to "automated participants"; automated market makers sometimes need "a few seconds" to reprice after live-game shocks. Polymarket charged zero trading fees on NBA markets during the sample. Post-game, MMs withdraw quotes (median spread 7,532.65 bps post-game vs. 1,030.90 bps in-game) — [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1)
- Polymarket order lifecycle docs: "Sports/game delay: enabled on configured sports markets around live game conditions. The order waits for the market's configured delay window before matching"; crypto/finance up/down markets hold marketable orders 250 ms. Delays apply to marketable (taker) orders, not resting orders — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/order-lifecycle)
- Polymarket help center: a 3-second delay on placement of marketable orders in sports markets; a 1-second taker delay "currently being tested on NBA and MLB markets"; outstanding limit orders are automatically cancelled once a game begins — [Polymarket help: Limit orders](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364444-limit-orders)
- A secondary NBA guide claims "automated court-side bots routinely exploited this latency gap to snipe stale resting limit orders from retail traders" (no incidents cited; treat as unverified commentary) — [Virlan](https://www.virlan.co/prediction-market/nba-kalshi-polymarket-prediction-market-partnership-cftc-rules/)
- Developer post on a Kalshi–Polymarket cross-venue arb bot: in one 24-hour window logged 870 cross-venue price gaps with a median time open of ~9 s; "no sizable arbitrage found" after fees — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/michaelmustopo/built-a-prediction-market-arbitrage-no-sizable-arbitrage-found-36cf)
- Polymarket adopted Palantir/TWG Global monitoring software for its US sports markets (Mar 10, 2026) — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/03/10/polymaket-palantir-sports-betting-screening)
- Kalshi–Genius deal: Kalshi joins Genius's integrity information-sharing and "certain market types will be restricted" — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/genius-sports-signs-kalshi-and-polymarket-as-us-prediction-markets-scale)

**Historical courtsiding and sportsbook defenses**
- Daniel Dobson, 22, arrested at the 2014 Australian Open; prosecutors alleged a device hidden in his shorts transmitted point outcomes "before they are available through official channels"; he worked for Sporting Data Ltd (Surrey); charge of "engaging in conduct that would corrupt a betting outcome" was dropped March 6, 2014 for lack of evidence; Sporting Data later dropped tennis courtsiding — [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/aust-open-courtsider-used-hidden-device/w0osgnenr); [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/uk-man-charged-courtsiding-australian-open/); [Wikipedia: Courtsiding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtsiding)
- FiveThirtyEight feature on the high-speed tennis betting ecosystem (courtsiders vs. feeds) — [FiveThirtyEight](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/inside-the-shadowy-world-of-high-speed-tennis-betting)
- Sportradar's Live Time Delay (LTD) API: an ML tool that, per live ticket, recommends whether to skip, apply, or increase the bet-acceptance delay; outputs `ltdSuggestion` (seconds, from coverage and player profile) and `ltdSuggestionEdl` (seconds, from an Event Data Latency model); inputs include Late Bet Score, Customer Confidence Factor, event risk profile, stake/market/match status, expected margin loss; high-risk players and new accounts cannot skip the delay; large-stake tickets get mandatory delay — [Sportradar docs](https://docs.sportradar.com/itservices/models/live-time-delay-ltd-api)
- Operator help page (Midnite): in-play markets are suspended to stop bets at stale odds and typically reopen within seconds; the book "will often impose a short bet delay on in-play bets" — [Midnite help](https://intercom.help/midnite/en/articles/2687791-in-play-betting)
- Altenar (sportsbook platform vendor) describes an acceptance algorithm that filters small odds changes so bets can be accepted at original prices after the delay — [Altenar](https://altenar.com/en-us/blog/sportsbook-features-guide-improved-bet-acceptance/)

### Inferences
- The UCLA result (in-game gaps closing in ~3.6 s, zero pre-game) is direct evidence that automated participants with fast data dominate in-game repricing on Polymarket; Kalshi, with SIG/Jump/sportsbook MMs and a dedicated low-latency feed, is unlikely to be slower.
- Polymarket's taker delay is explicitly a defense against fast takers picking off stale quotes; the fact that it is applied to marketable orders means the person being protected is the resting liquidity provider (usually the MM), not the retail taker. A retail trader's own resting limit orders remain exposed to faster participants.
- The absence of a Kalshi taker delay (none found) means Kalshi relies on MMs' own "order protection" tools and quote-pulling; this favors well-equipped makers over retail resting orders.

### Gaps
- No documented Kalshi-specific taker delay, in-game matching delay, or "speed bump" was found in Kalshi docs or filings. Absence of evidence, not confirmed absence.
- No named enforcement action, lawsuit or investigative report of courtsiding/latency arbitrage against Kalshi or Polymarket NBA markets was found (only the UCLA measurement and anecdotal X/Medium posts).
- Betfair's in-play `betDelay` (commonly described as 1–12 s and exposed in its API) was mentioned in search summaries but not verified against a primary Betfair page in this session.
- DraftKings/FanDuel specific in-play acceptance-delay values were not found in primary sources; one low-quality site claims a 1.4 s FanDuel median and should be discounted.

## KQ4. How Kalshi's live order book works; pauses/halts; position limits; treatment of winning accounts

### Takeaway
Kalshi runs a central limit order book with price-time priority, 1-cent ticks and a 1% minimum spread; roughly 85% of sports volume is in-game, where top-of-book depth is thin (about $4.1K per side near 50c in Blockworks' March Madness sample). Kalshi uses position accountability levels (e.g., $25,000 per strike per member on sports title futures) rather than hard limits on most contracts, exempts program market makers from position limits, and states it does not limit winners because it is an exchange; I found no documented case of Kalshi restricting an account for being profitable. Kalshi has halted/paused markets in documented non-sports and settlement-ambiguity cases and has had outages during live games, but no rule mandating in-game pauses on scoring plays was found.

### Cited Findings
**Order book mechanics**
- Kalshi's rulebook matches by price then time: orders at different prices execute best-to-worst regardless of time; same-price orders execute oldest first — [DeFi Rate explainer](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/how-order-books-work/); Kalshi CFTC rule filing of Nov 14, 2024 (not read directly) — [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules1114248723.pdf)
- Changing a resting order's price on Kalshi is cancel-and-replace (loses queue position); reducing size preserves priority (secondary source) — [Predictefy](https://blog.predictefy.com/can-you-change-the-price-of-a-limit-order-on-kalshi)
- Third-party developer docs report a weekly Kalshi maintenance window Thursday 03:00–05:00 ET during which order placement fails with TRADING_PAUSED (cancels still work); resting GTC orders survive pauses unless flagged `cancel_order_on_pause` — [Scalar Field](https://scalarfield.io/docs/trading/venues/kalshi)
- Kalshi sports: 1% price increments and 1% minimum spread; spreads tighten to ~1.11% about 48 h pre-game and sit near the 1% floor through completion; in-game trading ≈85% of total volume; depth rises 19x in the 48-h pre-game window but falls 76% in-game; in-game depth averages 16.3K contracts per market vs. a 73K pre-game peak; near $0.50, in-game touch depth ≈$4.1K per side (March Madness 2026 sample) — [Blockworks Research, Apr 16, 2026](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)
- Kalshi taker fee 0.07 × P × (1−P) per contract (peaks ≈3.5% of a 50c stake); maker fees reach ≈0.88%; effective round-trip costs in the 40–60% range 2.8–4.2%; Polymarket sports taker fee 0.03 vs. Kalshi 0.07 (as of Apr 2026) — [Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)
- Kalshi earned $2.3M in fees on the $171.6M Knicks–Spurs Game 4 winner market — [InGame](https://www.ingame.com/susquehanna-biggest-sports-kalshi-volume-knicks/)

**Pauses / halts / outages**
- Kalshi paused trading and settled at last price when Super Bowl halftime "performance" criteria were ambiguous (Cardi B) — [Closing Line (Substack)](https://closingline.substack.com/p/the-takeaway-kalshi-goes-down-pope-betting-markets)
- Papal-conclave markets: trading halt, then "orders can now be modified or canceled" at 12:14 pm ET with trading resuming a minute later; site and app also went down — [Closing Line (Substack)](https://closingline.substack.com/p/the-takeaway-kalshi-goes-down-pope-betting-markets)
- Kalshi outage during Bears–49ers (Dec 2025) blocked users from closing positions as markets moved — [PiunikaWeb, Dec 29, 2025](https://piunikaweb.com/2025/12/29/kalshi-outage-blocks-users-from-closing-positions/)
- Kalshi "temporarily pausing listing of announcer [mention] markets" on July 31 amid CFTC review — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/272940/report-kalshi-paused-sports-mention-markets-amid-federal-review/)

**Position limits / accountability**
- Rule 5.19 ("Position Limits," rulebook v1.18) defines a limit as "the maximum loss that can be incurred as a result of a position in a Contract"; 5.19(b) lets Kalshi liquidate positions over the limit; 5.19(f) aggregates across controlled accounts and groups acting under "an express or implied agreement"; 5.19(a) exempts program market makers — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-position-limits)
- November 2024 filing converted many contracts from hard limits to position accountability levels (Rule 5.18): crossing requires providing position info on request and halting increases if instructed; sports title futures ("Will <Team> win <Title>?") carry a $25,000 per-strike, per-member accountability level (Jan 2025 filing); most legacy contracts 25,000 contracts per strike per member — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-position-limits); Cornerstone lists a $25,000 Kalshi position limit — [Cornerstone Research PDF](https://www.cornerstone.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Event-Contracts.pdf)

**Treatment of winners**
- Tarek Mansour (RotoWire interview): on sportsbooks "you get limited, and you would get banned in ways that you basically would not on prediction markets because of that structural difference" — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/article/an-hour-with-kalshi-ceo-tarek-mansour-131979)
- Blockworks: sportsbooks "may limit successful users' stakes. Kalshi has no stake limits" — [Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)
- Aaron Brown (Bloomberg Opinion, Apr 13, 2026) argues the exchange business-model point does not settle the gambling question — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-04-13/kalshi-ceo-tarek-monsour-is-half-right-about-prediction-markets)
- Kalshi has fined and suspended three US politicians for insider trading (2026), showing accounts can be suspended for conduct — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/06/08/sportradar-prediction-markets-kalshi/)

### Inferences
- Because Kalshi earns fees on both sides and MMs are third parties, Kalshi has no bookmaker-style incentive to limit a profitable taker; the practical "limit" a winner faces is thin in-game depth (~$4K per side at the touch) and MMs widening or pulling quotes, not account restriction.
- MM exemption from position limits plus fee rebates and "order protection" tools are structural, disclosed advantages; the retail trader is a price-taker against them.

### Gaps
- No Kalshi document found that specifies in-game market handling on scoring plays (e.g., automatic halts or widened bands). Kalshi's contract-specific rules for NBA/NCAA game-winner markets were not retrieved.
- Per-game or per-member accountability levels for single-game NBA/college markets (as opposed to title futures) were not found.
- No documented instance (positive or negative) of Kalshi restricting a profitable sports trader was found; the "no limiting" claim rests on Kalshi's own statements and commentary.

## KQ5. Academic/industry evidence on prediction-market sports efficiency (Kalshi, Polymarket, Betfair) vs. sportsbook lines

### Takeaway
Kalshi in-game prices track FanDuel closely (correlation 0.996 and 0.974 in two Blockworks case games) with median gaps of ~1.6–2.6 points that sit inside total friction (~2.75%) most of the time but exceed it in 29–40% of five-minute windows; after taker fees Kalshi is not reliably cheaper than books. Polymarket NBA arbitrage is rare and closes in seconds. Betfair in-play literature finds prices react correctly to expected goals but overreact to surprises, with a 2026 preprint still reporting a positive ROI simulation. No peer-reviewed study benchmarking Kalshi/Polymarket against sportsbook closing lines was found.

### Cited Findings
- Blockworks Research (Kunal Doshi, Apr 16, 2026; 3.1K games, $8.5B notional; basketball + NFL = 72% of Kalshi sports volume): prices two days out deviate from realized outcomes by 5.5 points on average. Michigan–Arizona NCAA Championship vs. FanDuel: in-game correlation 0.9962; median in-game gap 2.25% (another passage says 2.59%); max 3.64%; 8 of 20 five-minute intervals (40%) exceeded the 2.75% friction threshold (FanDuel ~2.25% per side vig + ~0.5% Kalshi tick/spread). Buffalo–Denver NFL playoff: correlation 0.9738; median gap 1.61%, mean 2.40%, max 8.8% during momentum swings; 13 of 45 intervals (29%) exceeded 2.75% — [Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)
- Cheng, Yang, Zou (UCLA, 2026): Polymarket NBA inefficiencies "localized entirely within the live-game phase and corrected swiftly by automated participants," median 3.6 s — [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1)
- Angelini, De Angelis and Singleton, International Journal of Forecasting ("Informational efficiency and behaviour within in-play prediction markets," Betfair soccer, goals as news events): prices respond correctly when a goal is expected but overreact when it is a surprise; mispricing linked to a reverse favourite-longshot bias and larger for surprise goals (e.g., longshot scoring late) — [University of Reading](https://research.reading.ac.uk/football-research/new-journal-article-informational-efficiency-and-behaviour-within-in-play-prediction-markets/); [paper PDF](https://reading-9.eprints-hosting.org/98329/1/information_efficiency_angelini_de_angelis_singleton.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Blockworks in-game gaps (up to 8.8%) show Kalshi and FanDuel do diverge during momentum swings, so cross-venue in-game disagreement exists; but with ~$4K touch depth, 1% ticks and 3.5% taker fees, the exploitable portion for a retail taker is small, and nothing in these studies shows retail (as opposed to automated makers) capturing it.
- The Betfair literature's "overreaction to surprise events" is the one documented in-play inefficiency that a slower, judgment-based trader could in principle exploit, because it persists for minutes rather than seconds. Whether it exists on Kalshi NBA markets is untested.

## KQ6 (Objective synthesis). Can a retail trader watching TV ever be ahead of Kalshi's in-game price?

### Takeaway
On pure speed, no: official/in-venue feeds reach market makers in roughly 1–4 s, while the TV viewer sees the play 6–38 s later on cable/OTA and 45–60 s later on streaming, and Polymarket evidence shows automated participants reprice in-game within ~3.6 s. The only openings documented are non-latency ones: (1) market-maker model errors on extreme states (Knicks at 5% down 29), (2) overreaction to surprise events (Betfair literature), (3) cross-venue disagreement windows of 2–9 points in-game (Blockworks), and (4) outages/halts. Each is small relative to 1% ticks, 3.5% taker fees and ~$4K touch depth, and a retail trader's own resting orders are the stale liquidity that fast participants pick off.

### Cited Findings
- Feed latency: Sportradar "Low" <4 s — [Sportradar docs](https://docs.sportradar.com/live-data/latency-indicator-beta); venue-to-client <1 s company claim — [Fairplay Sports Media](https://www.fairplaysportsmedia.com/insights/sports-data-infrastructure/sports-betting-data-feed-integration-technical-guide)
- Broadcast latency (measured, Feb 2026): OTA ~19 s, cable 38 s, Peacock 48 s, YouTube TV/Hulu 53 s, DirecTV Stream 60 s, NFL+ 62 s — [TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/platform/study-ota-broadcasts-offered-lowest-latency-of-super-bowl-lx-feeds); older cable figure 6–8 s and NBA app 12 s (2022) — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/nba-streaming-latency-nba-app/)
- In-game repricing speed on Polymarket NBA: median 3.6 s for single-market arbs, closed by automated participants — [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1)
- Kalshi MMs include SIG, Jump, Flutter, DraftKings with fee rebates, order-protection tools and direct Sportradar contracting — [The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/); [Sportradar](https://sportradar.com/content-hub/news/sportradar-and-kalshi-announce-first-of-its-kind-data-and-infrastructure-global-partnership-for-prediction-markets/)
- MM mispricing example: Knicks ~5% down 29, below 10% for 80+ minutes, $22.4M MM loss — [InGame](https://www.ingame.com/susquehanna-biggest-sports-kalshi-volume-knicks/)
- In-game Kalshi vs. FanDuel gaps up to 3.64% (NCAA) and 8.8% (NFL) with thin in-game depth — [Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)
- Taker loss rate −1.12%/trade on Kalshi — [The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)

### Inferences
- A broadcast-derived signal (including computer-vision tracking of the TV feed) cannot beat official data on timing for any event the official feed records (scores, fouls, timeouts). It could only add information the feed does not carry or carries late: e.g., a visible injury before it is logged, lineup/garbage-time cues, or shot-quality/possession context that MM models may underweight. Even then the trader is acting 6–60 s after the event and must cross a 1-cent-tick book paying ~3.5% taker fees at mid-range prices.
- The sensible retail posture implied by the evidence is model-based (judging MM mispricing on extreme/surprise states) and maker-side (resting orders at prices you want, accepting adverse selection) rather than speed-based; the evidence does not show this is profitable, only that it is the sole lane not foreclosed by latency.

### Gaps
- No measurement of Kalshi's own in-game repricing latency after NBA scoring events exists in public sources; the 3.6 s figure is Polymarket.
- No measured 2026 cable/streaming delay specific to regular-season NBA/college basketball broadcasts; the Super Bowl numbers may overstate typical cable delay.
- Whether Sportradar/Genius cover lower-tier college basketball in-venue (low latency) or from TV (higher latency, per Sportradar's own indicator) was not established; this is the one place a TV-based retail signal could be closer to parity, and it is unverified.
