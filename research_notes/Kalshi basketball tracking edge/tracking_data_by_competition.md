# Basketball tracking-data availability by competition (as of 1 Nov 2026)

Scope note: Research was done via web search and fetches of primary press releases and trade press. Several primary pages (Sportradar/SportBusiness, Business Wire, SBC) returned 403/429 and could only be read through search-engine summaries; those cases are flagged. Anything dated before 2024 is labelled historical and its current status is stated as "unverified" unless a 2025-26 source confirms it.

Vocabulary used below:
- "Optical tracking" = in-venue multi-camera systems producing player/ball XY(Z) at 25-60 Hz (Hawk-Eye, Second Spectrum, SportVU-era).
- "Broadcast-derived tracking" = computer vision run on the TV feed (SkillCorner, Stats Perform AutoStats; Genius/Second Spectrum and Sportradar also have CV capabilities).
- "Wearable/LPS" = sensor-based (Kinexon UWB tags, ShotTracker ball/jersey sensors). Not optical, but equivalent positional output for the venue that has it.
- "Official data" in sportsbook contracts means play-by-play/box events from a stats platform (NCAA LiveStats / FIBA LiveStats / Sportradar scouts), NOT positional data, unless a source says otherwise.

## Key Question 1: Competition-by-competition map — which competitions have optical tracking, who receives it, and which have none (or team-only use)?

### Takeaway
Full league-wide optical tracking whose outputs reach data companies exists only in the NBA (Hawk-Eye + Sportradar + Genius), the WNBA and G League (Second Spectrum/Genius), and a handful of small leagues on Genius's GeniusIQ computer-vision stack (Lithuania LKL; B.League announced Feb 2026). EuroLeague/EuroCup, ACB, BBL, LBA, LNB, Turkish BSL, NBL, CBA and the vast majority of NCAA D-I games have no league-wide optical tracking; where positional data exists in those competitions it is wearable (Kinexon, ShotTracker) or club-purchased broadcast-derived (SkillCorner, AutoStats) and is not sold onward to sportsbooks.

### Inferences
- The only competitions where a data supplier to sportsbooks already holds a positional feed are the NBA (Sportradar, and Genius via its team-analytics/augmentation role), and the small Genius-CV leagues (LKL; B.League once deployed). For WNBA and G League, Genius holds tracking but the public announcements name teams/broadcasters only, so sportsbook delivery is possible but unconfirmed.
- EuroLeague is the largest competition with no league-wide optical feed: Sportradar's 2026-31 deal is explicitly a scouts/official-data + video rights deal, and SkillCorner's EuroLeague coverage is sold to teams. That makes EuroLeague/EuroCup, ACB, BBL, LBA, LNB and BSL the clearest "books have no positional data" set, with the caveat that Sportradar or Genius could run their own CV on the betting video they already receive at low latency.
- In NCAA, the hardware that exists (Synergy automated cameras in ~175 D-I arenas, ShotTracker in ~60 programs, Pac-12 GeniusIQ from 2026) is sold to coaches/scouts or conferences, not books. Sportsbooks receive LiveStats-style official data from Genius.

### Gaps

- Could not confirm 2025-26 status of G League (Second Spectrum, 2022) or any change to the WNBA provider after 2024; no 2025-26 announcements found.

- The Pac-12 GeniusIQ release's "tracking data" is not specified as optical; hardware unstated.
- Whether Synergy's Automated Camera System output (center-of-mass tracking) is in any way distributed to Sportradar's betting clients is unknown.

## Key Question 2: NCAA specifically — what do Genius, Synergy, Sportradar and ShotTracker actually provide, and do any sportsbooks receive positional data?

### Takeaway
Genius supplies NCAA LiveStats official play-by-play (not tracking) to sportsbooks for the postseason and to ESPN for ~65,000 games; Synergy/Sportradar sells logging plus automated-camera "center-of-mass" tracking to coaches and scouts; ShotTracker sells wearable/ball-sensor data to programs and conferences (Big 12, Mountain West); SkillCorner and AutoStats sell broadcast-derived tracking to teams. No source shows any sportsbook receiving NCAA positional data.

## Key Question 3: EuroLeague, domestic European leagues, NBL, B.League — tracking status and data-rights holders

### Takeaway

## Key Question 5: Broadcast-derived tracking products — what exists commercially and which leagues/books already use them?

### Takeaway
Broadcast-derived basketball tracking is commercially mature on the team side (SkillCorner: NBA/NCAA/EuroLeague, 25 fps, $60M raise Dec 2025; Stats Perform AutoStats: Orlando Magic since 2019) and Genius's GeniusIQ/"Dragon" uses cheap camera arrays (reported iPhones) to bring optical tracking to small leagues and FIBA events.
